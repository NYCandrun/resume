## 1. Overall portrait (calibrated)

**Moderate confidence.** In these excerpts Jacob is a talkative, explanation-first marketer. He asks what a piece of work is *for* before he builds it. He likes to explain products through concrete scenarios and everyday analogies, and he links product features to what a buyer or executive worries about. He is direct and often informal: masked profanity, sweeping generalisations, blunt statements about colleagues and vendors. He is also quick to apologise, take ownership of small mistakes and say he "misunderstood".

He judges his own skill highly and says so often ("my core competency", "I pride myself"). He is also open about specific limits, such as design, creative work and multitasking.

From 2025 H2 to 2026 the rough signals are:
- more first-person commitments and requests per 1,000 words;
- fewer "I don't know" hedges;
- a shift in what he says about his team using AI, from "quality … goes way down" to process-driven use.

**Low–moderate confidence** that these surface patterns reflect stable traits. That would need the missing context.

## 4. Strengths and possible friction

**Strengths visible in the text**
- Positioning and explaining: analogies, buyer-fear framing, and different stories for different audiences.
- Insisting on purpose and concept before production.
- Willingness to name his limits and apologise for small errors.
- Specific, reasoned praise at times, e.g. "you did a great job" with a reason [1 | 2025-06-10 | L6–7], and reassuring a frustrated colleague [12 | 2025-07-03 | L472].

**Possible friction (hypothetical effects, not observed outcomes)**
- Sweeping or blunt statements about vendors and colleagues *could* hurt trust with agencies or peers.
- Close-oversight habits (2.8) *could* feel like micromanagement to reports. He himself mentions people asking whether he thinks they are "incompetent" [34 | 2025-08-21 | L1927].
- Frequent confident self-assessment *could* read as defensiveness when his workload is questioned.
- Long, branching speech *could* make it harder for listeners to pull out decisions. Few explicit owners or dates appear.

## 5. Collaboration hypotheses (inferences only)

- He is likely to do best with collaborators who give a clear goal and success metric, since he asks for these himself.
- He probably responds well to direct feedback. The text shows quick acknowledgement ("I hear that"), but whether his behaviour actually changed afterwards cannot be seen.
- Agreeing explicit owners and dates at the end of a meeting would likely make up for the scarcity of named next steps.
- By 2026 he may be more open to AI-assisted team workflows if the work is well specified.

- **Search hits:** About 350 matching lines were shown individually.
- **Total:** About 1,000 lines were looked at (about 12% of 8,015). Roughly 700 were read in context (about 9%).
- **Important distinction:** The searches cover the whole corpus, but most passages were not read. The counts are lexical frequencies, not hand-coded behaviours.
