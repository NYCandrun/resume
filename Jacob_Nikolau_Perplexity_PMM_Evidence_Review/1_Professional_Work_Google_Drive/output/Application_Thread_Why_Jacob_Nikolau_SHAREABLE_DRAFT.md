**Thesis: Jacob builds positioning the way Perplexity builds answers. He starts from original research, says plainly what is live and what is roadmap, and checks his claims against the numbers, including when the numbers are unflattering.**

## Executive summary

Since 2021 Jacob has spent nearly five years in positioning-led B2B roles. He was the first product marketer at NovoEd, an enterprise learning platform, and the first marketer at Mosaic, an AI resource-management platform for architecture and engineering firms. Before that he spent three years in growth roles, so he has about eight years in B2B go-to-market overall.

His work follows a repeatable pattern:

1. Run primary research.
2. Turn it into a buyer-specific point of view.
3. Package it as launch kits and talk tracks that sales and customer teams can use.
4. Measure the funnel honestly.

He has positioned two AI products: an AI translation launch at NovoEd and Mosaic's AI planning agent, Chello. He also uses AI as an operator, building Claude workflows that mine sales calls for objections and proof points.

## Five examples

**1. Research as the raw material for positioning.** At NovoEd, Jacob helped run a survey of 515 learning leaders with Executive Networks. [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/learning-talent-development-pros-foresee-demise-in-person-corporate-training/635000/) covered the findings, and Jacob turned them into a bylined [Training Industry article](https://trainingindustry.com/articles/strategy-alignment-and-planning/capability-training-how-learning-leaders-are-adapting-to-changing-business-priorities/) on how learning leaders were adapting.

He also prepared a 127-participant customer value study with Cascade Insights. Its findings became a set of six value drivers, which then shaped the product feature taxonomy. The chain from research to messaging to taxonomy is visible in the artifacts.

**2. Launches built as kits, not one-off documents.** For NovoEd's Lesson Libraries release, Jacob co-authored the user stories with the product manager. Around them, the launch materials included:

- a phased-rollout FAQ
- an early-adopter scoring exercise
- an enablement deck
- customer email built from early-adopter data

For the AI translation launch he owned the positioning and benefit pillars, the "New Release" datasheet and the sales overview. A draft release-preparation process from 2023 adds launch tiers and a per-audience enablement matrix.

**3. Technical storytelling for an AI agent, with honesty built in.** At Mosaic, Jacob's sales talk track for Chello tagged every capability either "LIVE · JUNE 2026" or "ROADMAP Q3/Q4 2026". Each one paired a pain-led opener with a single discovery question.

That discipline matters for any AI product, where overclaiming is the fastest way to lose trust. He was a named co-author of Mosaic's [State of AI in A&E 2026](https://www.mosaicapp.com/whitepapers/state-of-ai-in-a-e-2026) report, a survey of 138 professionals that became a [three-part webinar series](https://www.mosaicapp.com/webinar/a-e-ai-adoption-trends-in-2026). He also appeared in [executive webinars](https://www.mosaicapp.com/webinar/ai-and-the-future-of-a-e-what-leaders-need-to-know-now) alongside Mosaic's CEO.

**4. Data-grounded narratives, including uncomfortable ones.** Jacob built Mosaic's deal-origin attribution model, a channel-by-channel audit of cost per qualified opportunity, and paid-channel reviews. One of those reviews reported, plainly, that a paid social channel had produced almost no qualified pipeline.

The habit it shows is the one a product marketing team at an answer engine needs: claims with sources, caveats kept, unflattering results reported.

**5. Influence without authority.** At NovoEd, a cross-functional sprint process put Jacob in a working group with customer success, solutions and product leads. He drafted positioning, gathered feedback from that group, and built the sales and customer-experience slides with the product manager.

He has also briefed and directed designers and agencies. At Mosaic he co-presented with the CEO and handed over a launch plan that clearly separated what had shipped from what was still on the roadmap.
